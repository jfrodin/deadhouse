# Vision and creative direction

*Status: approved direction (October 2026). This document describes intent. What actually exists is in
[STATUS.md](STATUS.md).*

## One line
A rain-soaked noir RPG in which a morgue attendant carries the last hours of the dead through a city that
sells them.

## The game in brief
**Corvane** is an invented city in an invented mid-century: streetcars, sodium lamps, radio, pneumatic
tubes, early television, glass towers above the smog and tenements beneath the elevated rail. It is
always night. **Ines Calder**, the night attendant at the city morgue (the "Deadhouse"), was a nurse
until she was struck off for stealing morphine. One night a corpse speaks to her, and from then on she
can take the **last hour** of the dead. This is a *Remnant*: a skill, a memory and a voice.

Bodies then start arriving **hollow**. Someone is harvesting the last hours of the dead and distilling them
into **Last Call**, a luxury that lets the rich live through other people's final nights. Ines can do
naturally what the drug does artificially.

## Pillars
1. **The city is the star.** Six districts, each with its own light, colour, architecture, people and
   sound. Corvane rewards curiosity whether or not the main story is involved.
2. **Light is gameplay.** Darkness hides you, lamps expose you, and breaking a bulb changes a room.
   Lanterns, matches and gunfire make you visible.
3. **Progression has faces.** Every Remnant is a person with a history, an opinion and unfinished
   business. Lessons are permanent traversal abilities, so you never backtrack just to swap them.
4. **Adult, not adolescent.** Desire, sex, sex work, addiction, violence, class and power are part of the
   fabric of the city, written with weight and consequence. Intimacy is real, but never explicit.
   Sexual violence is never shown and is never something the player does.
5. **Choices that cost.** No good-versus-evil toggles. Characters remember what Ines did, and the city
   changes between Nights.

## Tone
Beautiful, ugly, seductive and frightening at once. Warmth and humour sit against corruption and decay:
a dancer's joke on a smoke break, a projectionist who has screened the same reel every night for
twenty-three years.

## Visual identity — "noir duotone with district colour"
* World art in pixel form at 480×270, scaled exactly ×4 to 1080p. Characters are about 36 px tall.
* Everything is lit at runtime on the GPU: coloured lights, neon bloom, wet-street reflections, rain, fog,
  film grain.
* Each district owns a colour: Lantern Row is magenta and cyan neon, the Sumps sickly sodium, the
  Smelter Wards furnace orange, the Heights champagne gold.
* Conversation portraits use a separate high-resolution style, "ink and light": hard-edged light and
  shadow shapes with a neon rim.

## Audio identity
The diegetic band comes first. The Velour's house band bleeds through walls onto the street and plays on
apartment radios. The music is jazz built on instruments synthesis handles well (FM vibraphone,
plucked-string upright bass, additive piano, clarinet, brushes) and deliberately avoids the ones it
handles badly (a lead saxophone, vocals). Rain, the elevated train and the city are the bed beneath
everything.

## Scope of the full game (proposed)
Eight Nights, six districts plus interiors (Saint Agathe's, the Drowned Line, the Gilt House, a hotel, a
foundry, the Cut), about 10 Remnants, 6–8 major encounters, roughly 6–8 hours. The fallback scope is four
districts and six Nights.
